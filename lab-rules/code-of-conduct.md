# Code of conduct

> **Read this when** you join the lab, and again before you speak for the lab, spend its money, use its equipment or share its data.

This code describes how members of the Advanced Telerobotics Research Lab work together and represent the lab. It extends Kent State University's [Code of Student Conduct](https://www.kent.edu/studentconduct/code-student-conduct) (University Policy Register 4-02, Administrative Code 3342-4-02), and it applies to everyone in the lab: undergraduate and graduate students, staff, faculty, visiting researchers and program participants. Employees also follow the university's employee policies. Where this code and a university policy differ, the university policy wins. The one thing to remember: if you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask before you act.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Draft until the director approves.** This code is a draft for Jong-Hoon Kim, associate professor of computer science and director of the lab, to review. Until he approves it, treat it as guidance, and treat the Kent State University policies it links as binding.

## Eating and drinking

No eating or drinking in the lab, at the benches or at the desks. Food and drink near soldering, resin, batteries and open electronics is a safety hazard (see the [safety rules](safety.md#general-rules)), and a spilled drink can destroy a robot or a workstation. Eat and drink in [designated break area], wash your hands before you come back, and take food waste out of the lab.

## Integrity

Research depends on trust: in your data, your code and your words.

- **Report what happened.** Never fabricate data or results, never falsify them (for example by dropping trials that don't fit without saying so), and never present someone else's words, code, figures or ideas as your own. Kent State's policy on [misconduct in research and scholarship](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-misconduct-research-and-scholarship-0) (Policy 10-07, formerly 3342-3-05) applies to everyone in the lab, and its [procedures](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-and-procedures-regarding-allegations-and-instances-misconduct-0) (Policy 10-07.1) say how allegations are handled.
- **Label demos honestly.** Every video, demo and figure says whether the robot was teleoperated, autonomous, semi-autonomous or scripted, and whether a video is sped up. Never describe simulation results as hardware results.
- **Never upgrade a result.** A finalist is not a winner, and "in the lab" is not "in the real world".
- **Keep records.** Keep your code in Git, record how each result was produced and keep the raw data where the lab and the IRB protocol say.
- **Coursework is separate.** Using lab code or results in a class needs your instructor's and the director's permission, and Kent State's academic integrity rules apply.

The full research standards, including authorship, citations and AI use, are in [research standards](../research/standards.md).

## Support each other

The lab works because people help each other. Treat every member, visitor and study participant with respect.

- **Welcome newcomers.** Answer questions without judgment; everyone was new once.
- **Share what you learn.** Write it down in the project README or the lab's docs so the next person doesn't have to rediscover it.
- **Give credit.** Name the people whose code, data and ideas you build on, in talks, papers and pull requests.
- **Disagree about ideas, not people.** Critique work in a way that helps it improve.
- **No harassment or discrimination.** Kent State's policies on harassment, discrimination and Title IX apply everywhere lab work happens, including Slack, email and conferences. If something happens, see [reporting a concern](#reporting-a-concern).
- **Look after yourself and others.** If you or a lab mate is struggling, Kent State's Counseling and Psychological Services are at 330-672-2487 ([Kent State concerns page](https://www.kent.edu/concerns)).

## Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest is any personal or financial interest that could affect, or look like it affects, your judgment in lab work. Examples: evaluating a product from a company you work for or own shares in, reviewing a paper by a close friend, or supervising a relative.

- **Tell the director** about any interest like this before it affects your work, even if you think it doesn't matter. Most conflicts are easy to manage once they are known.
- **Employees** follow Kent State's [conflict of interest policy](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-conflict-interest-university-employees) (Policy 6-23), which bars being "in a position in which private interest conflicts with public duty."
- **Investigators on sponsored projects** must disclose significant financial interests under the university's [financial interest policy](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-financial-interest-sponsored-projects) (Policy 10-04) and its [procedures](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-financial-interest-sponsored-projects-0) (Policy 10-04.1).

## Outside employment

Jobs and internships outside the lab are fine, and often good for your career. Keep them separate from lab work:

- **Tell your advisor** about outside work that overlaps with your research area, so conflicts of interest and confidentiality can be handled early.
- **Don't use lab time, equipment, data, code or unpublished results** for an outside job without the director's written permission.
- **Read what you sign.** An employer's confidentiality or invention agreement can conflict with lab work; show it to the director before you sign it.
- **Faculty and academic administrators** must get approval for outside employment each academic year under Kent State's [outside enterprises policy](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-outside-enterprises-including-employment-faculty-and-academic) (Policy 6-24).
- **Graduate assistants and student employees:** check your appointment terms for limits on outside work: [where students check assistantship and employment terms].

## Business opportunities found through the lab

Lab work sometimes leads to opportunities: a company that wants to use a lab project, a consulting request, an idea for a startup, an invention.

- **Bring every opportunity to the director first.** Don't pursue it privately, and don't make promises for the lab or the university.
- **Inventions:** Kent State's [patent policy](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-patents-0) (Policy 10-06, with [procedures](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-regarding-patents-0) in 10-06.1) requires employees to notify the university of possible inventions by filing an invention disclosure. Talk to the director and Kent State's [Office of Technology Commercialization](https://www.kent.edu/research/technology-commercialization) (330-672-0701) **before** you present, post or publish anything that might be patentable; a talk or preprint can affect patent rights.
- **Sponsors and partners:** agreements with companies go through Kent State's Office of Sponsored Programs, and gifts through the Division of Philanthropy and Alumni Engagement. The director makes the first contact.

## Use of the lab's products and services

The lab's code, robots, datasets, website, printers and name belong to the lab and Kent State University.

- **Use lab resources for lab work.** Personal projects on lab equipment (including the 3D printer) need permission: [lab policy on personal use of equipment and printers].
- **Code and data:** lab repositories carry their own licenses. Don't publish lab code, data or models, or make a private repository public, without the director's approval.
- **Names and logos:** the lab's name, logos and roundel are treated as university trademarks. Don't put them on products, merchandise or other organizations' materials; lab merchandise needs approval from Kent State University Communications and Marketing (UCM) and a licensed vendor. The lab's design rules are in the [ATR Lab design skill](https://github.com/ATR-Lab/atr-lab-design-skill).
- **Software licenses:** education licenses (Autodesk, Unity and others) are for learning, teaching and educational research, not commercial work ([Autodesk Fusion and Inventor](../tech-stack/autodesk.md#get-an-education-license)).

## Outside communications

- **Media:** if a reporter contacts you, get their name, outlet, deadline and question, and pass it to the director. Kent State's University Communications and Marketing "is responsible for disseminating all official Kent State news and handling the university's media relations" ([UCM](https://www.kent.edu/ucm/how-university-communications-marketing-can-help)); the lab works with [UCM Media Relations](https://www.kent.edu/ucm/media-relations) on any story.
- **Don't speak for the university.** Only people Kent State authorizes speak for it. When you comment in public on your own behalf, make clear you speak for yourself, not for the lab or Kent State. Contact with government officials on the university's behalf is reserved for the president or a designee (Kent State [Policy 5-03.1](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-regarding-engagement-governmental-officials-and-entities)).
- **Lab channels:** the lab's accounts are on X (@atrlab_kent), Instagram (@atr_lab), GitHub (ATR-Lab) and YouTube ("Advanced Telerobotics Research Laboratory"). Only [lab social media administrators] post for the lab, following Kent State's [10 Required Elements](https://www.kent.edu/ucm/social/10-required-elements) for university social accounts. Don't create new accounts in the lab's name.
- **Results and news timing:** share results publicly only after they are published or accepted, and never break a journal embargo or a double-blind review ([preprints, embargoes and news timing](../research/standards.md#preprints-embargoes-and-news-timing)).
- **Personal posts about lab work:** no unpublished results, no photos of people without their consent, and never photos or names of minors.

## Lab equipment

Lab equipment is Kent State property, bought with grants and university funds, and shared by everyone.

- Use a device only after training, and check it out and back in ([equipment checkout](best-practices.md#equipment-checkout)).
- Don't take equipment out of the lab without a recorded checkout and the approval of [lab manager name].
- Don't open, modify or reflash a robot or instrument (firmware, wiring, mechanical changes) without the approval of the person responsible for it.
- Report damage, loss and malfunctions right away. Accidents happen; hiding one makes it worse.
- Lab equipment must be cleared by Kent State EHS before it is thrown away or moved to another location ([moving lab equipment](https://www.kent.edu/facilitiesplanning/moving-lab-equipment)); ask [lab manager name].

## Spending the lab's money

Only the director approves spending lab funds, and every purchase follows Kent State's purchasing rules.

- **Ask first:** send a purchase request through [purchase request procedure] with the item, vendor, price, quantity and the project or grant it is for. The director decides which fund pays.
- **Kent State buys through FlashCart.** A requisition entered in FlashCart becomes a purchase order after approval, and "a requisition should be completed before goods or services are obtained" ([Procurement](https://www.kent.edu/procurement/it-okay-obtain-goods-and-services-prior-getting-po)). FlashCart is required for goods of $2,500 or more, and goods of $25,000 or more must be bid ([purchasing takeaways](https://www.kent.edu/procurement/what-are-some-key-takeaways-purchasing-policy)).
- **No personal purchases** with university funds, by purchase order or purchasing card ([Policy 7-12.1](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-regarding-purchasing-goods-andor-services); [P-card policy 7-02.16](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/administrative-policy-regarding-use-purchasing-card-p-card)).
- **Don't pay with your own money** expecting to be reimbursed unless the director has approved it in writing beforehand: [reimbursement procedure].
- **Grant funds** can only be spent on what the grant allows; the director and the Office of Sponsored Programs decide.

The lab's own after-hours rules: [after-hours rules, for example no robot testing or soldering alone].
- **Leaving:** the last person out turns off soldering irons and chargers, shuts the door and checks that it locks.

## Privacy

- **Research data:** data about people, including study data, recordings and logs, is stored and shared only as the IRB protocol and Kent State's [data handling policy](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-data-handling) (Policy 9-04) allow ([data management](../research/standards.md#data-management)).
- **Study participants:** never share a participant's identity or data outside the approved study team, and never use study photos or video in a talk, post or demo unless the consent form covers that use.
- **Photos and video:** Kent State asks that photos not name students without their written permission ([UCM web images](https://www.kent.edu/ucm/web-team/images-graphics-and-video)), and people who are not Kent State students or employees sign a model release before their photo is used ([UCM photography](https://www.kent.edu/ucm/photography-and-videography)). Ask before you photograph anyone in the lab.
- **Minors** (high school interns, workshop participants, visiting children): a signed parent or guardian photo and video release is required before any image of a minor is used, and minors are never named or tagged. Kent State's [policy on activities involving minors](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-regarding-campus-activities-involving-minors) (Policy 5-19) also bars one-on-one electronic or social-media contact with minors unless there is a clear educational or university-related purpose.
- **Student records:** grades and other student records are protected by FERPA. Never share another student's records.
- **Robots record too:** cameras and microphones on lab robots capture visitors. Tell people when a robot is recording, and don't keep footage of people who have not agreed.

## Password sharing

- **Never share your Kent State credentials.** Kent State's [responsible use policy](https://www.kent.edu/policyreg/university-policy-responsible-use-information-technology) (Policy 9-02) says users may not "share authentication details or provide access to their university accounts with anyone else (e.g., sharing the password)." That includes your FlashLine password and your two-factor codes.
- **Shared robot and service accounts** (a robot's onboard computer, a lab server, a shared cloud account) are managed by [lab manager name] in [lab password manager]. Get access there, never by message.
- **Never put a password, key or token in a repository**, a README, Slack, an issue or a prompt to an AI tool. If one leaks, tell [lab manager name] so it can be changed.
- **Change default passwords** on every robot and device before it goes on the network, and change shared passwords when someone leaves the lab.
- **If your account may be compromised**, contact Kent State Information Security (<security@kent.edu>, 330-672-5566) ([Secure IT](https://www.kent.edu/secureit/fraud-recovery)). Forward phishing email to <phish@kent.edu>. For other help, TechHelp is at 330-672-HELP (4357).
